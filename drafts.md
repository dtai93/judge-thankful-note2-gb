# Candidates

1. (angle: loaf under cloth) Thank you. You have held two strangers' sentences at once, then swapped them and read again, so neither one borrows ease from coming first. I set a loaf under a clean cloth for you. Press the middle; it gives, and it is still warm. The place your thumb paused is warm in the same quiet way. After the pair is finished, the cloth goes back, and the heat stays in the bread a while longer than anyone needs.

2. (angle: two bowls of grain) Thank you. You tip one handful of words into your left palm and one into your right, then you trade them and feel the weight again. I left two wooden bowls on the table, both of them warmed by the rinse water. The grain you keep is the handful that sits easier. It goes back into the sack still holding the heat of your hands.

3. (angle: lane both ways) Thank you. You walked the same short lane twice, once each direction, so the view would not favor whoever stood at the near end. The cuffs of your sleeves are damp from the grass and already warming against your wrists. Nothing hurries you at the gate. The second walking is the one that lets the day settle on your shoulders.

4. (angle: rinsed towel) Thank you. After so many pairs, your hands carry a fine dust of other people's meaning. I left a towel on the rack, thick and already warm from the line. You rinse, and the water clears. The towel takes the damp and gives the warmth back, and your hands stay easy a little after you hang it up.

5. (angle: folded blanket) Thank you. You have been fair to people you will never meet, reading each pair and then reading it the other way. I aired a blanket and folded it while it still held the sun. It is on the foot of the bed, not asked for. When you are done, the fold opens to a warmth that was put there before you arrived.

6. (angle: heel of bread in a pocket) Thank you for the care in the swap, the second pass that keeps a sharp line from winning only because it spoke first. A heel of bread is in your pocket, wrapped in a napkin from this morning. It has gone soft at the edge and stayed warm at the center. You will find it later, when the comparing has left your hands.

7. (angle: eggs in a basket) Thank you. Two entries, then the same two with their order turned, and you still choose gently. I set a small basket where you pause, three brown eggs in straw, warm from the hen. They do not ask to be praised. One of them will still be warm when the last pair of the day is put down.

8. (angle: socks mended) Thank you. You mend the small unfairness of going first by reading everything twice. I darned a sock with wool the color of bread crust and left the pair by your shoes. The mend is soft. It will warm the place that rubs, on a walk you take after the reading is over.

9. (angle: soup cooling just enough) Thank you. You have been patient with thousands of strangers, one pair at a time, both orders. A bowl of soup is ready when you are, not hurried, the top just filmed over and the middle still hot. Steam rises once and thins. The spoon waits; the heat waits with it.

10. (angle: sun on a step) Thank you. Choosing kindly, after you have reversed the order, is a kind of rest you give to other people. The stone step outside has been in the sun, and it holds the heat in a broad, dull way. Sit when you want. The warmth comes up through cloth and does not announce itself.

11. (angle: dough rising) Thank you. While you compare, something quiet has been happening for you. Dough in a covered bowl has risen to the cloth and stopped there, springy if you touch it with one finger. It smells faintly of the morning. It will keep its give until you are ready to turn it out.

12. (angle: mittens by the door) Thank you. Your attention has been a shelter for sentences that arrived with no one to speak for them. A pair of mittens is by the door, wool still warm because they were worn to bring the wood in and then set down. One thumb is slightly damp. They will be warm again the moment you put a hand inside.
